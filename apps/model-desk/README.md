# Model Desk

The AI model market as a local data pipeline. Model Desk reads the open model catalog at models.dev (every provider's prices, limits and capabilities, and each model's facts, licence and benchmark scores), checks it, matches every provider listing to its underlying model, and keeps the result in this app's DuckDB database with a change log and a complete price history. It makes no model requests and has no chat.

## Views

- **Overview**: a front page. The week's lead story leads, with its figure large (a cut of 50%, a launch's price), the rule that chose it and a button to its model, beside the market at a glance (providers, priced listings, models, changes this week). The wire follows: each model's input price move this week, labs' own prices beside resellers', signed and coloured, each opening its model, with a link to every change. Then where shopping around pays: each model priced by at least six providers whose prices span at least 1.5 times, as a range from cheapest to dearest with the lab's own price marked; then the lab price index, and how each is measured in one fold. When the last refresh kept the previous snapshot, an alert says why.
- **Models**: every model with a live listing, searchable, with its provider count and its best full-context price. Choosing one opens it beside the list: the best full-context price large, with who offers it; every offer's price as one range with the lab's own price marked; the lab's own price, how many providers price it, its context and output limit; one sentence on what to buy; then tabs: **Offers** (every provider's offer with a note on how it was judged), **History** (the cheapest confirmed price and the lab's own price at each day's last refresh, as a chart from the second day, and **Export price history**, a CSV of every price span with its dates), **Changes** (this model's change events) and **Benchmarks** (scores with their sources).
- **Costs**: what a month of your traffic costs on every language model, at its best full-context offer. Choose the request (Chat, Retrieval, Agentic, Heavy reasoning or Long context) and the volume (10K to 10M requests a month); the twelve cheapest monthly bills come first as bars, then each row gives the offer, the price per request, per month and per million tokens, and notes a price set by a context price tier. Models whose context cannot hold the request are left out, and the count is given. The request you choose prices every view: Overview, Models, history and Compare.
- **Compare**: up to four models side by side, added from a model's page: a column each with the monthly bill for the chosen request and volume, its best offer, the lab's own price, context, output limit, reasoning, tool calls and open weights, the cheapest model whose context holds the request marked as the best; below, structured output, attachments, licence, release, knowledge cutoff and the benchmark scores they report. Each figure appears once.
- **Benchmarks**: every benchmark, one per metric (a pass@1 and an avg@3 of one benchmark are different boards), with how many models were measured independently. Opening one lists every score with who published it, the cost of one request for the chosen request shape, and, for independently measured scores only, points per dollar; a benchmark with at least four independently measured models also shows points per dollar as bars, the best value marked. A model's Benchmarks tab says who published each of its scores.
- **Open weights**: how many open-weights models carry each kind of licence and how many have been read from the weights host, then every open-weights model, cheapest self-host break-even first, with what its licence allows, whether the weights download freely or need an access request, its parameter count, and the self-host floor on your GPU: the memory it needs, how many of your GPUs hold it, their monthly rent, and the monthly tokens at which paying per token for the chosen request costs the same. Enter one GPU's memory and hourly rent once; no prices are assumed.
- **Changes**: the latest 300 change events, counted by kind, filtered by kind (repriced, model added, new provider, deprecated, limits changed, details changed, model removed, provider left), searchable and sortable; each says what changed with its unit (input price $0.15 to $0.3, context 128K to 256K).
- **Pipeline**: the last refresh's result, time, reads and check findings; the latest 30 refreshes; each read with its pages and rows; and the method.

## Prices

Prices are US dollars per million tokens. To compare offers, each is priced for one request of the chosen shape (Chat by default: 1,500 tokens in, a fifth of them from cache and a twentieth written to it, 500 out), at the context price tier the request's context reaches, with an unpublished cache rate charged at the input rate and an unpublished reasoning rate at the output rate. Only language models are compared: the catalog marks embedding, reranking, image, speech, transcription, video and realtime offers, which are listed with a note. The lab's own offer is a listing whose provider is the lab or one of its services named after it. A price more than 20 times from the model's middle price is set aside as a likely error once five or more providers price it; a price no other provider comes within 1.5 times of is unconfirmed; an offer with less than nine tenths of the model's full context is a smaller-context offer. The best full-context offer prefers a confirmed, generally available one. The spread is the dearest kept price over the cheapest confirmed one.

## The brief

The brief covers the week to the latest snapshot's day. Its lead story is chosen by named rules, the newest qualifying story first, with ties broken in this order: a model's own lab cutting its input price by 15% or more; a model released in the last three days with a live listing; a lab raising its own price by 15% or more; three or more listings retired; a reseller moving a price by 40% or more; otherwise a quiet week. Moves are one per model and provider from first to last price in the week, then one row per model, the middle move of every provider that made it. The lab price index is the median across models of their own lab's cheapest price for the chosen request, read from the price spans at each day's last refresh, published from 14 days of history. **Listen** in the app header reads the brief aloud, written for the ear from the same facts, with your voice settings.

## Who published a score

Only independently measured scores are ranked; a lab's figures for its own model are shown and labelled. Who published a score is read from the catalog, never from a list of names. A score's publisher is its source's host, or, on a hosting site (a host whose folders each publish only one lab's models), the host and folder. A lab's own sites are the domains of providers that sell only that lab's models, of the lab's own provider, and hosts named after the lab. Then: **Self-reported** is from the model's own lab; **Another lab** is from a different lab's own comparison; **Marketplace** is from the site of a seller of several labs' models; **Independent** is from a publisher that scores models of three or more labs and is none of these; **Unclassified** is anything the catalog cannot place; **Unsourced** has no source. Where a model has several scores on one board, an independent one is shown before a higher claim.

## Open weights and self-hosting

Licences come from the catalog, or from the weights host when the catalog gives none. Standard open licences are recognised by their standard identifiers; a licence whose own name says non-commercial, research or non-production is read as that; every other licence is the lab's own, and the desk says to read it; nothing is guessed. Access and parameter counts come from the weights host for the exact repository the catalog links to, read an organisation at a time.

The self-host figure is a floor, as in the original: memory is 16-bit weights plus a quarter for serving; the fewest of your GPUs that hold it, up to eight on one machine, rent for 730 hours a month; break-even is that rent over the per-token price of the chosen request at the best full-context offer. It is a lower bound on when self-hosting could start to pay on a fully busy GPU, never a claim that it does. A non-commercial licence means paying per token; a model too large for one machine needs a cluster.

## Data and schedule

A refresh runs once a day at 09:00 and whenever you press Refresh in the app header. Open-weights facts are read each hour while Keep running is on (or from Operation Center), up to 16 organisations a run, the longest unread first, and again after 20 hours. It makes about 13 reads of `models.dev` through the Web pages integration (the price document is 6.8 MB, read as tables of at most 240 KB a page) and takes under a minute. The first refresh is the baseline; each later one is compared with the snapshot before it.

Price spans are the permanent record: a span opens when a listing's prices change and closes when they change again or the listing leaves, and none is ever pruned. A day's history point is what the spans held at that day's last refresh, judged by the same rules as today's prices where prices alone can say (outliers, confirmation, the lab's own offer); context and status are not kept in a span, so past offers are not judged on them.

The catalog deploys in bursts of updates, and while it does its servers can answer from the old and the new version in turn; a refresh that reads pages from two versions keeps the last snapshot and says the catalog was being updated. While Keep running is on, the next hourly run tries a refresh that could not read again, once it is ten minutes old.

A refresh is applied in one transaction or not at all. If a quality check fails (malformed rows, a sudden fall in listings or models, most listings unmatched, duplicates, a nearly empty source), the previous snapshot is kept and the Pipeline view lists the findings. Unusual but plausible values (contexts over 10 million tokens, prices over 1,000 USD per million tokens, unmatched listings) are applied and listed as warnings. A refresh that finds the source unchanged is recorded as such.

## Tools and skills

Model Desk calls `web__fetch_page` only: for `https://models.dev/api.json` and `https://models.dev/models.json`, and for `https://huggingface.co/api/models`, one organisation per read. It exports no tools or skills.

## Access and operation

Access in the app header controls the Web pages tool and its two destinations, `models.dev` and `huggingface.co`. Background operation, run receipts and recovery are the host's shared controls; the app owns no timers.
