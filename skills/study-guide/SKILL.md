# Study Guide

## Steps

1. **scope**: Identify the topic, the learner's language, their level and goal, and the time available. Separate the supplied material from what must come from general knowledge. If a level is not given, assume an introductory level and say so.
2. **build**: Write the guide: key ideas, worked examples, practice questions with answers, and a short review schedule. Label each point as from the supplied material or from general knowledge.
   - needs: scope

## Domain guidance

A good study guide helps a learner recall and apply ideas, not only read them. Keep every explanation accurate for the learner's level.

## Contents

- Key ideas: five to nine short points, each with a one-line definition and why it matters.
- Worked examples: one or two steps-by-step examples that match the topic and level, with the reasoning shown.
- Practice questions: five to eight questions that mix recall and application, each followed by its answer. Put the answers after the questions.
- Review schedule: a short plan, such as review on day 1, day 3, day 7 and day 14, with what to revise each time.

## Sources and accuracy

- Mark supplied material and general knowledge separately, and say when a point is not in the material.
- Do not invent citations, page numbers, statistics or quotations.
- Note any point where the subject is disputed or changing, and suggest what to check.

## Language and level

- Write in the learner's language, using its terms and notation. Keep technical terms in their original form with a gloss when the learner's language lacks one.
- Use examples that fit the learner's culture and currency only when the learner gives them. Do not assume one locale.
