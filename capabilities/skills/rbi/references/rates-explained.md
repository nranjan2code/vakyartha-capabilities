# RBI rates in plain language

Fixed explanations for "explain simply" answers. Always add the current value and date from `get_rbi_rates`.

- **Policy repo rate:** the rate at which RBI lends overnight to banks against government securities. It is RBI's main policy signal; when it falls, loan rates linked to it (many home loans) usually fall after a lag, and when it rises they usually rise.
- **Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) rate:** the rate RBI pays banks that park surplus money with it overnight, without collateral. It is the floor of the overnight rate corridor.
- **Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) rate:** the rate at which banks can borrow overnight from RBI in an emergency, above the repo rate. It is the ceiling of the corridor.
- **Bank rate:** RBI's rate for longer-term lending to banks; it is aligned with the MSF rate and used for penalties.
- **Fixed reverse repo rate:** the older rate RBI pays for absorbing money through reverse repo; now secondary to the SDF.
- **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR):** the share of deposits a bank must keep with RBI as cash, earning nothing. A higher CRR leaves banks less to lend.
- **Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR):** the share of deposits a bank must hold in safe assets such as government securities, cash or gold.
- **Reference exchange rate (FBIL):** the benchmark rupee rate for a currency, published once each working day by Financial Benchmarks India (RBI shows the time with the rate). It is a reference, not the rate a bank or money changer gives.
- **T-bill cut-off yield:** the yield at the last auction of 91, 182 or 364-day government Treasury Bills.
- **Call rate:** the range of rates at which banks lent to each other overnight that day.
- **Base rate and MCLR:** banks' internal minimum lending rates; RBI shows the range across major banks.
- **Savings and term deposit rates:** the range banks pay depositors; RBI shows the range across major banks.
